# Gotchas, in full

Every trap this project has hit, with what was measured and how it was found. `CLAUDE.md` carries a one-line rule for
each; this file is the reason behind the rule. Add new ones at the bottom with the date, and add the one-line rule to
`CLAUDE.md`.

- ffmpeg single-image input breaks with `-fflags nobuffer`; apply it only to `udp://` video. Never on the audio UDP input: it discards what ffmpeg reads while probing and ate the first sentence. `hearing.js` uses `-probesize 32 -analyzeduration 0` instead. The JPEG encoder needs `format=yuvj420p`.
- **`-ac 1` must be an output option in `audiod.sh`.** On the input side ALSA ignores it and ffmpeg streams stereo; `hearing.js` reads the stream as mono, so whisper got half-speed interleaved garbage all day (2 Nov) while `arecord` clips on the Pi transcribed perfectly. Diagnose this class of bug by recording the same moment on both ends (`ffmpeg ... -f s16le udp://... -f wav /tmp/tee.wav` on the Pi) and comparing with `ffprobe`.
- The Pi USB mic rejects 16 kHz in ffmpeg: `audiod.sh` captures at 48 kHz and resamples. Select it by name (`plughw:CARD=Device,DEV=0`); USB card numbers change after reboots.
- **Never pass `--prompt 'Hey Peak.'` to whisper-server.** Whisper treats the prompt as text already said and suppresses it, so the wake word was being cut off the front of every utterance ("Hey Peak, wave at me" came back as "Wave at me."), on both `small` and `medium`. Without the prompt `medium.en` hears "Hey Peek" reliably, which the filter accepts.
- Whisper hallucinates on quiet audio: "Thanks for watching.", `[BURP]`, bare "Hey.", and whole fluent sentences nobody said. The earlier regex-only filter also dropped the wake phrase itself. Now `heardFilter.js` gates on the wake word; do not add "hey peak" back to a drop list.
- `PEAK_MIN_DB` has no good fixed value on this mic (2 Nov bake-off recording): at `-30`, 4 of 10 phrases spoken at normal distance never opened the gate ("Stop." and the quieter ones); at `-38`, room noise never closed it, so every utterance ran to the 12 s cap, one phrase was cut mid-word across two clips, and whisper returned `[Silence]` for the empty ones. Mean level of real speech clips is -30 to -33 dB, idle about -36. Proper fix: a real VAD (whisper.cpp ships Silero: `--vad -vm ggml-silero-v5.1.2.bin`) or an adaptive noise floor, not another constant.
- `hearing.js` SIGKILLs its ffmpeg child on exit; with SIGTERM it lingered and kept UDP :5001 bound through a quick restart.
- When restarting a sense for a test, kill the mind too or check `pgrep -f orchestrator/index.js`: two minds answer every event twice (happened 2 Nov).
- Do not `pkill -f` on the Pi over ssh with a pattern that appears in the ssh command line itself; it kills the session. For `pgrep`, bracket one letter (`pgrep -f "[r]picam-vid"`).
- Starting a Pi daemon over ssh with `cd dir && VAR=x setsid nohup cmd > log 2>&1 < /dev/null &` hangs the ssh call: the redirects cover only the last command, and the `&&` list's subshell keeps the session's stdout open. Background one plain command (no `cd &&`), as in the Commands table.
- A raw H264 UDP stream has no container, so ffmpeg probes it for its full 5 s on every open. `vision.js` passes `-f h264 -probesize 200000 -analyzeduration 500000` for `udp://` sources and a look takes 2 s. `camd.sh` sends a keyframe every second (`--intra 15`) so a fresh open never waits long for one, and runs `rpicam-vid -n -v 0`, because by default it logs a line per frame (15 a second, to the SD card).
- Only one program on the Mac can read the camera's UDP :5000 at a time. With `ffplay` open, a second ffmpeg fails with "bind failed: Address already in use" (checked 5 Nov 2026), so `vision.js` gets no frames while a viewer is up, and a viewer fails to open while `vision.js` is mid-look. To watch and run vision together, `camd.sh` would have to send to two ports (or `vision.js` would have to save its frames for viewing).
- `say` on the Pi goes to `espeak-ng` as an argv entry, never through a shell (injection fix). Keep it that way.
- The Pi shows **undervoltage** (`vcgencmd get_throttled` = `0x50005`) and has hung or rebooted under load. Still present on 2 Nov 2026 after a new supply AND with the USB mic unplugged: dips every 10-30 s at idle, CPU pinned at 600 MHz. So it is the cable or the supply itself, not a peripheral. After a cable swap (2 Nov evening): idle is clean (one dip at boot, then none over 30 s), but 25 s of 4-core load still produced a dip and live throttling (`0x50005`, CPU at 600 MHz). So the cable was part of it; the supply is still marginal under load. Real-load test (mic streaming + hearing + mind, 4 min, 2 Nov evening): 1 logged dip, no reboot, but the live undervoltage bit was set at the end and the CPU sat at 600 MHz, so the rail hovers right at the 4.63 V threshold. Usable for bench work; not trustworthy for camera + servos. Next: measure the 5V GPIO pin under load (want 4.9-5.2 V); if under 4.8 V, use an official-grade 5.1 V 2.5 A supply. The battery + UBEC path will replace this anyway.
- Mini560 comes in 3.3/5/9/12 V fixed variants; check the SKU says 5V, then measure with a multimeter before connecting the Pi.
- **`qwen2.5vl:3b` is 10.4 GB resident**, not 3.2: the vision encoder and image KV are allocated on load. "8B brain + 3B eyes" was about 16 GB, the whole GPU budget, which is why the 14B never fit and why the other program's `qwen3:14b` evicted our models. Judge vision models by `ollama ps`, not `ollama list`.
- Gemma 4 (`huihui_ai/gemma-4-abliterated:12b` and `:12b-qat`, 7.6 GB each, removed 4 Nov 2026) was scored on 3 Nov 2026 after the Ollama upgrade: 94% strict but 3.0 s per reply, because llama.cpp re-evaluates the whole prompt every call for its sliding-window attention (Qwen3-VL reuses the cached prefix in 0.1 s). It also invented a person in the empty car interior. Not a candidate.
- `node --test <dir>` runs `orchestrator/index.js` as a test and hangs on MQTT. `npm test` names the test files; keep it that way.
- Prompt rules interact: adding a head-turn rule made the brain point at a man pointing, a yes → nod rule overrode "stay still" until that got its own rule. Never ship a prompt edit without `npm run bakeoff:brain -- --stream` and a look at the per-group table.
- 5 Nov 2026, the same lesson twice while adding sensed events. The rule "a sensed event gets one short line" made the brain narrate camera frames it used to ignore ("Screen's gone dark."), which the bake-off could not see because it only scored gestures on `see` events; cases can now say `"silent": true`. Then a rule telling it to stay silent on scenes silenced "Thanks Peak, good job" in 3 of 3 runs. What worked was putting the no-narration wording in the sentence that explains `see` events, plus one silent example, instead of a rule.
- And a third time the same evening, adding the eyes field: v1 of the prompt lost the wave on "night night" (the brain treated sleepy eyes as the whole answer); a goodnight example fixed it, and the example swapped in beside it ("Point at the door and look surprised") made "Look surprised" a `look_left`. A third run with the goodnight example alone showed that it, not the swap, makes "Rho, what are you?" wave: its gesture is a wave, and the model leans on it. An example teaches more than the thing it was written for. Change one example per run, or the cause stays hidden.
- **An SSD1306 OLED can be dark while every write succeeds.** On 5 Nov the owner saw nothing on it although `servod` logged no error: the panel's status byte read `0x42` (bit 6 = display off). Two things switch it off behind a running daemon: a power dip resets it, and any other luma program (the wiring guide's script A, a one-off test) blanks and switches it off as it exits. `servod` now reads the status byte before each draw and every 5 s from the sense loop, and redoes the full setup when the panel is dark (`[servod] display on` in the log). Check by hand: `SMBus(1).read_byte(0x3C) & 0x40`. Do not run another OLED script while `servod` is up and expect its picture to stay.
- **Never send the OLED whole frames for animation.** Measured on the Pi, 5 Nov 2026, 100 kHz I2C, undervolted: a whole 1024-byte frame takes 0.166 s to send, so 4 frames a second with the bus busy 68% of the time. `node/oled.py` keeps a copy of what the panel holds and sends only the changed bytes through column/page windows: the same eyes run at 14 frames a second, about 55 bytes and 16 ms a frame. The copy is thrown away after every re-init and once a minute, because the panel's memory cannot be read back over I2C (so what is on the glass can only be checked by eye or with `eyes_preview.py`). Sweat is the one animation that never stops, and on this power supply it is always on: alone it is drawn at 8 frames a second (measured 9, 0.17 s of sending and about 6% of a core per 10 s; `servod` as a whole 15%). `dtparam=i2c_arm_baudrate=400000` in `/boot/firmware/config.txt` would make every send about four times shorter (needs `sudo` and a reboot; not done). A frame can still hold the bus longer than a 20 ms servo step, and the PCA9685 will share it, so on real servos (not in sim) the eyes jump to where they were heading and keep still during a move. Untested until the PCA9685 is wired.
- pyatv 0.18 and a HomePod, two traps (5 Nov 2026). `pip install pyatv` pulls `srptools` 2.0, whose `SRPClientSession` takes `private` as keyword-only, and every connection dies with a `TypeError`; `voice/requirements.txt` pins `srptools<2`. And a plain connect (or `atvremote stream_file`, even with `--protocol raop`) also opens the AirPlay remote-control channel, which the HomePod refuses without credentials: "Failed to set up remote control channel". Scan with `protocol={Protocol.RAOP}` so only the audio service is in the config.
- **Rho is a hard wake word.** It is one syllable and sounds exactly like row and roe; the one bare "Rho" caught on 5 Nov came back from whisper as "Roll.". `heardFilter.js` therefore wakes on "rho" anywhere, but on the homophones (row, roe, ro, roh, rowe, roll, and since 7 Nov evening bro) only where a name stands: opening the utterance, with or without "hey", or closing it after a comma, and with punctuation after it. "Row wave at me" with no comma is missed; "three in a row" does not wake. The old name and its spellings (Peak, Peek, Pete, Pig) no longer wake it.
- The brain copies the few-shot example lines verbatim ("Hello there!", the weather line) even when told not to. Harmless, but vary the examples if it gets annoying.

## Added 5 Nov 2026, from a read of every past session

- **Several Claude sessions share this checkout.** On 2 Nov one session's commit swept up another's uncommitted bake-off files, and a pattern kill at the end of the day took another chat's processes with it. On 5 Nov a session found another's uncommitted `brain.js` edits in the tree, two pushes carried another session's commits, and a session was refused when it tried to stop a mind it had not started. Check `git status` and `git log` before committing, commit only your own files, and stop processes by pid.
- **Processes started from a Claude session die with it.** The mind, senses, voice and whisper server are background tasks of whichever session ran them. The robot going quiet after a chat is closed is this, not a crash.
- **A faked `heard` event is spoken aloud** on the Bedroom HomePod while `npm run voice` is up.
- **The shell's working directory drifts**, and parallel shell calls race on it: a script failed with `FileNotFoundError: CLAUDE.md`. Use absolute paths.
- **`grep` in a pipeline block-buffers** and showed false 20 s gaps in a live stream. Use `--line-buffered`.
- **An apostrophe inside `${VAR:?...}`** broke `camd.sh`.
- **Homebrew Mosquitto ignores `conf.d`** (`include_dir` is commented out in `/opt/homebrew/etc/mosquitto/mosquitto.conf`), so the listener lines are in the main conf.
- **Python on the Pi needs `-u`**, or the `[sim]` and `[servod]` lines never reach the log.
- **Stop commands over ssh hang when the Pi is down** (2 Nov, after a power dip).
- **Timed "speak now" recordings start before the owner has read the message.** Two captures on 2 Nov were silence. Use the Start/Stop page (`npm run record`) or cue him first.
- **Mic levels on this mic:** the room is about -36 dB mean and -25 dB peak; good speech peaks at -5 to -14 dB; a clip peaking at -0.3 dB came back from whisper as fragments.
- **Hot-plugging the USB mic** took the Pi's dip count from 1 to 4 and set live undervoltage (2 Nov 18:20), so on this supply the mic is a real load, whatever the earlier "not a peripheral" note says.
- **Synthetic speech flatters a speech engine:** macOS `say` clips scored 1.1% word errors where real Pi-mic clips scored 4.5 to 5.6%.
- **`/api/ps` can under-report:** it showed 0.4 GB for a model whose runner held 7.7 GB. Use `ollama ps`.
- **OpenClaw loading `qwen3:14b` in the middle of a bake-off** gave "fetch failed" and reloads. Rerun after it expires, and stop the mind and vision for timing runs.
- **`think:false`** may only be sent to models with the `thinking` capability; Ollama rejects it otherwise.
- **A schema `maxLength` can be ignored by the model**, so `parseReply` clamps `say` to 200 characters.
- **On Ollama 0.40 a 640 px frame is 1103 image tokens** (506 on 0.24); shrinking to 448 px changes nothing.
- **`cases.json` and `sequences.json` use indent 2;** a scripted rewrite once reformatted both.
- **luma calls `Image.getdata()`, which Pillow 14 removes.** The Pi has Pillow 13.
- **Trick steps go out at QoS 0,** so one can be lost.
- **Most of `servod`'s CPU is the sensor thread,** not the display.
- **The Mac venv is Python 3.9.6** (Pillow 11.3) and the Pi's is 3.13.5; `node/` code has to run on both, and numpy is on neither.

## Parts and wiring advice from the shopping sessions (2 Nov 2026)

Given when the parts were chosen; none of it has been tested, because the AliExpress order has not arrived.

- **GeekServo:** the "360°" listing is continuous rotation and cannot hold an angle; the 270° one is right. Rated about 4.8 to 6 V, so a 6 V or 12 V UBEC variant cooks them. The listing had 3 reviews: bench-test each servo before it goes into the build.
- **Six servos can pull 3 A or more** (0.5 to 0.7 A each under load), so the 3 A UBEC is at its limit on the servo rail.
- **The 3 A UBEC** (U-Angel) is 5.25 V ±0.5 V, so up to 5.75 V; 3 A continuous, 4 A for 20 s. Use it for the Pi only if it measures under about 5.4 V.
- **Mini560:** the 5V and the PRO 5V boards have `OUT+` on opposite sides.
- **Micro-USB pigtail to the Pi:** 2-pin, as short as possible, 22 AWG or thicker; a Pi 3B draws up to 2.5 A.
- **Charger board:** the 2S / 8.4 V variant only (3S or 4S damages the cells), fed from a 2 A or better USB-C charger.
- **Battery:** 2S of INR18650-30HW, 8.4 V full, about 6 V empty. Set the BMS cutoff at 6 V or higher; below about 7 V the Mini560 cannot hold 5 V for the Pi. The cells are unprotected: keep them bagged until the holder, BMS and buck are here. Check flat-top against button-top for the holders.
- **KCD1 switch:** rated 6 A.
- **Battery divider:** 10k over 4.7k, because two 10k resistors would put 4.2 V on the ADS1115, above its 3.3 V limit.
- **NeoPixel ring (16 LEDs):** about 1 A at full white. Power it from the servo rail at low brightness; 3.3 V data is marginal. It has bare pads, so it needs soldering.
- **Fan:** PH2.0 plug, needs re-terminating for the Pi header.
- **Camera:** leave a slack loop for the head servo. The 200 mm cable may not reach from head to backpack and no longer one was bought.
- **No M/F jumpers were bought:** the M/F pack was swapped for F/F on the assumption that no breadboard would fit in the backpack.
- **Rejected:** NoIR camera (purple colours in daylight), HQ camera (no lens, heavy for a 9 g servo), ReSpeaker 2-Mics pHAT (covers the GPIO header).
- **The USB soldering iron** probably needs a USB-C PD charger of 20 W or more.
- **If a good supply and cable still leave the Pi undervolted,** the Pi's aged polyfuse is the next suspect.

## Added 7 Nov 2026 late, from the example-bank bake-off

- **An aborted Ollama request cancels the model load it started.** `/api/embed` with a 400 ms `AbortSignal.timeout` against a cold `qwen3-embedding:0.6b` logged `Load failed ... context canceled` on every try, so the model never came up, every pick fell back to words, and after about 40 minutes of this Ollama answered 503 to every embed for the rest of the run (756 of 756 picks lexical in run 3). Load a model with no timeout first (`warm()` in `examples.js`; `npm run teach` and the mind do), then put timeouts on the calls that use it.
- **A third model evicts one of the two.** `OLLAMA_MAX_LOADED_MODELS=2` holds the brain and, while the desk camera runs, its 4B vision model. The embedder made three: 6 brain reloads in one bake-off run and `model predicted to exceed available memory, evicting` at 3.7 GB system free (`system_limited=true`, 24 GB Mac). Nothing else may load a model while the camera is on.
- **A prompt that changes per event loses Ollama's prompt cache.** Prompt eval 133 ms with one fixed system prompt, 309 to 714 ms when the examples differ per event; p50 0.7 s went to 0.9 to 1.3 s although the prompt was 400 tokens shorter. Fewer tokens is not faster when the prefix changes.
- **The desk camera serializes with the brain.** Each description takes 6.5 s on the shared model; with it running, live replies in `mind/run/mind.log` took 10 s (prompt eval 2.8 s) and a 756-call bake-off 80 min instead of 12. Stop the vision process (its pid is in `mind/run/vision.pid`) for timing runs, on the owner's word if another session started it.
- **One dropped connection ended a 756-call bake-off at call 220** ("fetch failed", nothing in the Ollama log). `run.js` now retries a call three times, 3 s apart, and counts retries in the summary.
- **A bake-off scores whatever is in `mind/examples/learned.yaml`.** The lessons are part of the prompt, so a baseline run after teaching is not the old baseline. `npm run teach` lists them.
- **Trimming the brain's history one turn per event defeated the prompt cache** (8 Nov 2026). `trimHistory` kept the last 20 messages, so from the eleventh event on it dropped the oldest turn every time. llama-server then matched only the system prompt (`cached n_tokens = 2130` in the Ollama log) and re-read all the history after it: prompt eval 1.87-1.99 s against 0.16-0.19 s, gesture out 2.0 s against 0.32 s, over 20 faked events through the live mind. This is the 4.5 s live median of 7 Nov. Now the history is cut back to the last 10 messages once it passes 20: one slower call (1.1 s to the gesture) in six.
- **On a Mac short of memory, two models evict each other** (8 Nov 2026). With 17.6 GB of swap in use, Ollama found 5-10 GiB free and would not hold the brain (6.3 GiB predicted) and the 4B desk model (2.7 GiB) together: from 07:24:20 to 07:26:09 each load evicted the other, every 8 s, and replies took 13-20 s. Once both fitted, gestures came out in 0.33-0.44 s with 145 looks running. OpenClaw's coder model evicts the brain the same way (07:20:31). The protection planned is the brain on its own Ollama instance; until then, read `system_limited` and `evicting` in `/opt/homebrew/var/log/ollama.log` before blaming the code.
- **A process that turned SIGTERM into a silent `exit(0)` left no trace** (7 Nov 22:09, vision). Every Mac part now logs `exiting on SIGTERM` or `exited (code N)`.

  pip-installs it from GitHub's release URL on the spot. On 8 Nov at 11:49 that URL answered 504 for minutes. A voice
