# Review of the fix brief, 9 Nov 2026 (Claude Code)

The brief was checked against `main` at `349d07d`. Its line numbers match the code. Baseline: `npm test` reports 112 passing (the 89 in `CLAUDE.md` is out of date). Scope here is items 1 to 3 only.

## Item 1: stop matcher

| # | Finding | Change to the plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1a | **The brief contradicts itself on negation.** Its "keep the fast lane's NEGATION exemption as it is" collides with its own test case "no no stop". `NEGATION` contains `no`, `in`, `after`, `again` and `leave`, so applying it to stops would reject "no no stop", "stop, again" and "stop in there". Today the exemption is skipped for stops (`fastlane.js:93`); "don't stop" is turned away only because it is not a template. | A narrow negation of the stop itself: `don't` / `do not` / `never` / `not` / `can't` / `won't` / `didn't` / `shouldn't`, then at most one filler word, then stop, freeze or halt. "Don't stop", "don't ever stop" and "I can't stop laughing" are not stops; "no no stop" is. |
| 1b | **"enough" as the last word is too broad.** "Is that enough?", "I've had enough" and "good enough" would all become emergency stops. ARCH did not list it; the brief added it. | `enough` counts only as the first word ("enough", "ok enough", since `ok` is stripped first, and "enough already"), or as "that's enough" / "that is enough". |
| 1c | **Compound nouns.** "Where is the bus stop" ends in "stop". | Not a stop when the word before it is `bus`, `full`, `pit`, `truck`, `non` or `door`. A false stop is cheap but not free: it cuts Rho's line and drops the reply being thought. |
| 1d | **An existing test changes.** `fastlane.test.js:40` expects "Can you stop the music?" to go to the brain. Under the brief's rule it becomes "stop the music", first word stop, so a stop. That follows ARCH's "a false stop costs nothing", and Rho controls no music. | The case moves to the stop list, with a comment. |
| 1e | **The gap the brief misses: the wake gate drops broad stops before the mind sees them.** `heardFilter.consider` drops "wait, stop" (no name) outside the 8 s attention window, and drops a bare "Stop." too, unless it lands near Rho's line. A long story outruns the 8 s window, and a servo move with no line spoken never opens the half-duplex bypass. Once servos are real, "Stop!" during a gesture would be dropped by hearing. A broad matcher in the fast lane alone does not reach these. | `consider` passes a stop intent with `why: 'a stop'` and does **not** open the attention window. It runs after the echo filter (as the brief wants for the broad matcher), so Rho's own voice still cannot stop Rho. The test that "Stop." does not *wake* the body stays as it is and still passes. |
| 1f | **The module layout needs the normaliser.** `isStopIntent` needs `normalize` (wake word, politeness), which lives in `orchestrator/fastlane.js`. If `lib/stop.js` imports it from there, there is an import cycle. The wake-word sound-alikes are also duplicated between `fastlane.js:14` and `heardFilter.js:19` (the same drift risk as the stop matcher). | A new `lib/utterance.js` holds `SOUNDS_LIKE`, `TAGS` and `normalize`. `lib/stop.js` holds `isStrictStop` (moved verbatim from `heardFilter.isStop`, re-exported under the old name) and `isStopIntent`. The seven stop templates leave the fast lane's map; `isStopIntent` is the only stop path there. |

## Item 3: hearing queue

| # | Finding | Change to the plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3a | **Short-first can publish out of speech order.** Example: a long request, then a short "yes", while whisper is busy. The mind then receives "yes" first. This is harmless in practice: the mind answers one event and the later one waits. It is rare, since it needs two utterances queued behind a transcription of about 0.5 s. | Kept as the brief says. Each event's `at` stays the publish time, as today. |
| 3b | The overwrite (B3) is the real bug; short-first only saves the stop about 1 s behind two long segments. Both are cheap, so both are done. | `senses/utteranceQueue.js`: pure `push`/`next`, `MAX_WAITING = 3`, short ≤ 1.5 s first, then FIFO. When full, the longest of the waiting items plus the new one is dropped, so a short one never goes while a long one waits. |
| 3c | The current `handle` re-enters itself for the waiting item without `await`. It works, but it would get tangled with a queue. | A small `pump()` loop drains the queue. |
| 3d | "Replay the 144-mix harness": those mixes are single utterances (echo plus request in one clip), so they do not exercise the queue. Replaying them also needs the running whisper server, which this session is not touching. | Not run. The live barge-in window is the real check. |
| 3e | S7's `node` field on queue items: hearing serves one `PEAK_NODE` per process, so the node is fixed at publish. | Not added. |
